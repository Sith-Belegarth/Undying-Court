# The Undying Court

*A Sith One-Shot — A 5e-Compatible Module*

**DM Eyes Only.** This document contains the full module: dungeon layout, encounters, boss stats, and campaign hooks. If you're a player, stop reading — spoilers ahead.

---

## How to Use This Booklet

This is built to run as a single satisfying session. Descent, dungeon, boss, done. Every section is also written so you can keep pulling threads afterward. Look for **Design Note** call-outs throughout; they mark places where the one-shot quietly opens a door into something longer, or explain the reasoning behind a room, encounter, or choice.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Party size** | 3–6, levels 3–5 (pregens included are Level 3) |
| **Session length** | One 3–4 hour sitting |
| **Tone** | Gothic ruin-crawl. Sith relics, undead royalty, corrupted grandeur. |

## Dungeon Flow

The critical path runs Antechamber → Sewers → Dining Hall → Ritual Hall → Crypt. Everything else is a genuine branch the party can skip without missing the boss fight — use this to keep your bearings mid-session.

## Scaling This Module

This module is written for 3–6 players, level 3–5 — but a party of 3 level 3s and a party of 6 level 5s are not the same fight, even against identical stat blocks. Look for **Scaling Note** callouts throughout; they call out the encounters where this matters most and give concrete adjustments per party size and level.

| Situation | General Approach |
|---|---|
| Small party (3), low level (3) | The module is tuned for this end of the range. Run encounters mostly as written; the boss fight and both sconce encounters are the ones to watch — they're the module's real difficulty spikes at this tier. |
| Large party (5–6), low level (3) | More actions per round than the encounters expect. Add one extra combatant to group fights (Guardians, Champions) rather than buffing individual stat blocks — keeps the action economy honest without inflating any single monster's HP. |
| Any size, higher level (4–5) | Static DCs and flat damage numbers age fast. Raise save DCs by 2–3, add a damage die to hazards (sconces, traps), and let Malqor use his higher-level spell slots — he's already statted as a 9th-level caster on the Mage block, so the tools are there. |
| Large party (5–6), higher level (4–5) | Combine both adjustments above. This is the module's easiest matchup by a wide margin as written; without adjustment, most encounters (including the boss) drop from Deadly/Hard to Easy. |

## Part 0: The Premise

The Undying Court was built by a King and Queen who foresaw the rising of the light and wished to prepare for it. It was both a grand palace and dangerous fortress. They repurposed their palace into a tomb so they might rise again when the balance of the dark began to overpower the light once again. Over the years, their oppressive, corrupted grandeur has turned to filth and ruin.

They chose undeath to master the dark side and cheat their own mortality. The Court styled itself as Dark Side adepts — Sith, in all but name — who mastered undeath itself as a weapon in their pursuit of immortality. Lichdom and vampirism weren't curses that befell them; they were the final rites of that mastery, chosen and pursued.

The party has their own reasons for descending — see the Rumor/Hook Ideas below, one per class, already written to slot straight into your pregens. Whatever brought them here, they all converge on the same partially-collapsed entrance.

### Rumor / Hook Ideas (by class)

| Class | Hook |
|---|---|
| **Barbarian** | This arena once crowned legendary blood-sport champions. If the monarchs return, you aim to be the next. |
| **Fighter** | A mercenary company vanished here centuries ago. A warlord offers gold or command of their own company for any of their lost ancestral blades. |
| **Cleric** | The altar once fed a dark god with blood rites. It will still answer to sacrifice. Your god commands you to cleanse it. |
| **Rogue** | The thieves' guild whispers of crystal-forged heirlooms and untouched gold. You paid dearly for the vault's key and need the investment to pay off. |
| **Wizard** | The old arcane orders lost the knowledge to craft a crystal-powered magic sword. The knowledge (and the crystals) may rest in the treasury. |
| **Bard** | Writer's block. Your muse only lights the way to inspiration in old ruins, dark and dangerous. |
| **Paladin** | You've spent your life building yourself as a leader. Your goal is to take control of the dark altar to call followers to you. |
| **Warlock** | The dark power once worshipped here still stirs, whispering: feed the altar a fresh sacrifice and the world receives dark miracles again. Obey, or resist? |
| **Ranger** | Legend speaks of a crimson stag cursed to haunt the ruined palace gardens. Its blood might grant immortality. You want it as a pet. |
| **Druid** | The Court's corruption spreads into the natural world. Blighted beasts and blackened vines creep outward. Restoring or destroying the Court's heart could rebalance the land. |
| **Sorcerer** | The last bearer of your bloodline's magic was famous for a crystal-powered sword. Finding one would let you live up to your lineage. |
| **Monk** | A scroll here describes the "Path of Darkness Made Flesh," a forbidden discipline blending body and shadow. Do you seek it to complete your art, or destroy it? |

> **Design Note.** Every hook above survives the dungeon. The warlord still wants that blade. The guild still wants its cut. The dark power that whispered to the Warlock doesn't go silent just because the module ends.

> **Design Note.** Payoff locations, at a glance: Barbarian and Fighter — Dining Hall trophy rack and the Ossyrran Blade (the Crypt). Rogue, Wizard, Sorcerer — the Treasury and/or Malqor's Study. Monk — the Treasury's scroll. Cleric, Paladin, Warlock, Druid — the Ritual Hall altar (fed or cleansed). Ranger, and Warlock/Druid again — the Inner Courtyard, off the Dining Hall (Part Three). Bard is intentionally the loosest hook in the set; Serai Thalos's own pregen Bond does the heavy lifting for her specifically, via the Sunken Hearth.

## Part One: The Descent

Regardless of how the party chooses to delve into the mysterious ruins, they attempt to go through the entrance first. They find it partially collapsed and have to squeeze their way into the crumbling grand antechamber.

### Grand Antechamber

**Read-aloud — quick version:**
> A vaulted hall, cold and damp, lit by the uneasy red glow of shattered sconces. Broken statues and a corroded, blood-streaked grate ring a floor littered with bones and faded mosaics. There's no obvious way through.

*Expanded, if you have the room for it:* Once a grand hall but now… a vaulted hall, abandoned. The air is cold and damp. Faded mosaics decorate the cracked stone floor. Scraps of molded tapestries still cling to the walls. A corroded grate yawns in the center of the floor, streaked with dark stains. Condensation trickles down the walls and pools across the slick stones, carrying the faint smell of rot. Red crystals flicker weakly from shattered sconces, throwing the chamber into an uneasy glow. Broken statues loom along the edges of the room. Some faceless, others toppled. Chains and bones litter the floor, silent reminders of grim ceremonies past. From above, loose stone groans softly in the vaulted ceiling. There appears to be no way through.

### Dungeon Features (Antechamber, Sewers, Dining Hall)

- Most rooms are dimly lit by crystal-powered sconces that function as turrets, dealing 1d4+4 necrotic damage every round until their crystal is removed. Once removed, the room goes dark.
- A phosphorescent moss grows throughout the sewers, dining hall, and antechamber. DC 10 Nature check reveals it flourishes best fed by blood but can survive on decaying flesh.
- The whole complex smells of decay, rot, and mold from ruined tapestries, carpets, and old carcasses.
- High ceilings echo. At the start of combat, choose ten random map areas. Roll a d10 at the end of every round of combat to see if that section's ceiling falls (see *Falling Rubble*, below).
- Perception checks are at disadvantage without darkvision throughout the dungeon — call this out early, it matters for the sconces.

> **Foreshadowing.** The red crystals powering every sconce in the dungeon are the same substance as the crystals the Wizard and Sorcerer pregens are hunting for their crystal-powered sword hooks. A harvested sconce crystal (DC 14 Arcana to recognize it, or the party just asks Selvos/Zyra) is a legitimate lead toward the Treasury (Part Three), not just a disabled trap.

### Combat: Grand Antechamber

`PUZZLE` · `DEADLY IF FOUGHT HEAD-ON`

**Magical Sconces**
Fire arrows of necromantic energy from the two red, glowing crystals in the shattered sconces until the crystals are removed or destroyed.

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| Initiative | +3 |
| HP | 80 |
| AC | 5 |
| Range | 25 ft. |
| Attack | +4 to hit |
| Damage | 1d4+4 necrotic |
| Fire Rate | Every round |
| Recharge | Round 5 (no actions) |

**Disable.** DC 14 Arcana or Dexterity (Thieves' Tools). On a failure, the crystal explodes for 8 necrotic damage (DC 14 Dex save for half).

> **Design Note: Sconces.** A creature that stays outside the sconce's 25 ft. range and out of direct line of sight is never targeted. Parties who scout before entering (Perception/Investigation to spot the crystals from the doorway) can route around both sconces entirely rather than fighting or disabling them.

> **Scaling Note.** Fought head-on, two sconces swing from Deadly (party of 3, level 3) down to Easy (party of 6, level 5) — the same stat block, radically different threat depending on your table.
>
> | Party | Suggested Adjustment |
> |---|---|
> | 3 players, level 3 | Run as written, or drop to 1 sconce if your table is cautious — two is genuinely Deadly if fought instead of bypassed. |
> | 4–5 players, level 3 | Run as written. |
> | Any size, level 4–5 | Add a third sconce, or raise damage to 1d6+4 per hit, to keep the room a genuine threat rather than a formality. |
> | 6 players, any level 3–5 | Add a third sconce covering the room's far corner — two sconces can't meaningfully threaten six actions per round. |

`EASY`

**Broken Guardians.** Worn statues, animated by the Court's residual dark side energy. Clamors to life when an adventurer comes within 60 feet, demanding the passerby swear to rise in resistance against the light (x1 guardian). It grinds and clamors as it attacks, bellowing fealty to the King and Queen of Undying Darkness.

*Note:* eight statues line the chamber. Only one attacks now — a warning shot, meant to foreshadow the rest rising in greater numbers later. Two more Guardians (reskinned) appear as Undead Arena Champions in the Dining Hall/Arena.

*Suggested statline:* use the **Animated Armor** stat block (AC 18, HP 33 (6d8+6), immune to poison/psychic and most conditions, Multiattack: two Slam attacks, 1d6+2 each). Reflavor as crumbling stone rather than metal.

> **Design Note: Broken Guardians.** A creature that speaks the oath of fealty aloud (even falsely) causes the nearest guardian to pause instead of attacking. Contested Deception vs. the guardian's passive Insight (10): success and it stands down for the encounter; failure and it attacks with advantage on its first hit, having caught the lie.

> **Scaling Note.** One guardian is Easy at every party size 3–6, level 3–5 — it's meant to be a taste, not a fight. Scale the taste, not just the later payoff:
>
> | Party | Suggested Adjustment |
> |---|---|
> | 3 players, level 3 | Run as written — one guardian. |
> | 5–6 players, any level | Two guardians instead of one; still a taste, but registers against more actions in the room. |
> | Any size, level 4–5 | Add a Multiattack bonus action (a third Slam at disadvantage) to keep pace with better AC/saves. |
>
> A convincing oath is remembered. Reference it if the party returns to this room later.

### Hazards & Interests: Grand Antechamber

- **Wet Floors.** DC 10 Dex (Acrobatics) to avoid slipping prone while dashing.
- **Pressure Plate (near grate).** Hidden beneath pooled water and mushrooms. DC 14 Investigation/Perception to notice. Trigger releases necrotic dart from an old arrow slit. 1d10 necrotic, +4 to hit.
- **Falling Rubble.** Random chance each time combat or a loud action occurs (10% per round, d10). Falling stone strikes one creature in a 10 ft. radius. DC 13 Dex save or 2d6 bludgeoning.
- **Drain Grate.** DC 5 Perception to notice. Lifts to drain grate to sewers. DC 18 Strength check for one creature, or two creatures can group check (both DC 12).
- **Collapsed Stairs.** DC 5 Investigation/Perception reveals that at some point someone on the second story threw large rocks down the stairs (possibly to repel invaders), collapsing them. Climbing requires DC 15 Athletics. On a success, a creature clears half the debris before the stairs collapse further and the creature falls (1d6 bludgeoning, prone). This also triggers a Falling Rubble check.
- **Mosaics.** Arcana or Religion DC 13 reveals the cracked, faded mosaics show blood sacrifice ceremonies, necromantic rituals, and blood sports — a clue pointing toward the Dining Hall/Arena.

## Part Two: The Depths (Sewer Tunnels)

The only way to continue past the grand antechamber is through the large drain and into the sewers. Navigating the old, overflowed sewage system requires some swimming, some Constitution DC 10 saving throws, and the possibility of trench foot.

*(See Map 01 — Sewers.)*

**Running this room:** The sewers are mostly a gauntlet, not a set-piece fight. Consider 1–2 short encounters (rot-touched vermin, a lurking ooze grown fat on the moss) plus the swimming/Con save gauntlet described above. If your table wants combat here, reuse the Broken Guardian stat block once, damp and rusted.

`RESOURCE DRAIN`

*The Sewers aren't meant to threaten a TPK — the swimming, Con saves, and short fights below are meant to cost HP, spell slots, and resources before the Dining Hall, not to be a hard fight on their own.*

> **Scaling Note.** DC 10 Con saves are real friction for a level 3 party and background noise for level 5 characters with better Con saves and more HP to spare.
>
> | Party | Suggested Adjustment |
> |---|---|
> | Level 3, any size | Run as written — DC 10 costs real resources here. |
> | Level 4–5, any size | Raise the swimming save to DC 12–13, or add a second save partway through, so the gauntlet still drains something. |
> | 6 players, any level | Add a second short encounter (vermin or ooze) — more actions in the party means the sewers clear faster than intended otherwise. |

**Rot-Touched Vermin (Sewer Swarm)**
A churning mass of disease-bloated rats, boiling up out of a side pipe when the party's noise (or blood) draws them. Use SRD **Swarm of Rats** as-is; the "rot-touched" flavor is cosmetic.

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| CR | 1/4 |
| AC | 10 |
| HP | 24 (7d8−7) |
| Speed | 30 ft. |
| Attack | +2 to hit |
| Damage | 2d6 piercing (7d4 if swarm HP ≤ half) |

Swarm can occupy another creature's space and vice versa; resistant to bludgeoning/piercing/slashing; immune to being frightened/grappled/paralyzed/petrified/prone/restrained/stunned. Half damage once reduced below half HP (no swarm below 1 creature's worth of space).

*Prefer individual bodies instead of a swarm stat block?* Use 3–4 SRD **Giant Rats** (CR 1/8 each; AC 12, HP 7 (2d6), Bite +4 to hit, 1d4+2 piercing; Pack Tactics) instead.

**Moss-Fed Ooze**
Grown fat on the sewers' phosphorescent moss, this ooze blends seamlessly with wet stone until something brushes past it. Use SRD **Gray Ooze** as-is.

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| CR | 1/2 |
| AC | 8 |
| HP | 22 (3d8+9) |
| Speed | 10 ft., climb 10 ft. |
| Attack | +3 to hit |
| Damage | 2d6+1 acid |

**Amorphous.** Can squeeze through a 1-inch gap. **Corrode Metal.** Any non-magical weapon that hits it corrodes; on a hit against the ooze, metal armor/weapons touching it take a cumulative −1 penalty unless they're immune to acid damage. **False Appearance.** Indistinguishable from a wet patch of floor or stone while motionless.

### The Sunken Hearth (Sewers Side-Chamber)

Reached through a second, smaller drain the party isn't required to take — a genuine branch off the main gauntlet, not a detour that gates progress. If your table wants a prompt to find it: a DC 12 Perception check while wading through the tunnels notices a current of clean, cold air where there shouldn't be one.

**Read-aloud — quick version:**
> The tunnel narrows, then opens into a low hollow that has nothing in common with the sewers behind you — older, rougher stonework, and a basin of black, still water at its center. Alcoves ring the walls, each holding a sealed clay urn, save for two standing empty near the entrance.

*Expanded, if you have the room for it:* Once you're through the collapsed drain, the tunnel narrows, then opens into a low hollow that has nothing in common with the sewers behind you. The masonry here is older — rougher, rounder, without the Court's sharp right angles. A basin of black, still water sits at the chamber's center, long dry of whatever spring once fed it, its rim carved with vine and hoof motifs worn nearly smooth. Alcoves ring the walls, each holding a sealed clay urn — save for two, near the entrance, standing empty. Someone built the Court's foundation directly into and over this place, and didn't bother to ask what it was first.

**What this room is.** Long before the King and Queen raised their palace, this was a sacred spring, tended by a satyr community who lived and died here in the open — pyres, not tombs. The Court's builders walled the spring into a cistern and used the older stonework as a foundation without ceremony. The urns are grave goods, not treasure in the dungeon's usual sense: ash, gold funerary masks, jewelry, and coin left for the dead rather than the living.

*The empty alcoves.* A DC 13 Investigation or Perception check notices the dust pattern is wrong at the two empty slots — disturbed recently, not centuries ago, and the urns that stood there are simply gone. No further explanation here; the party won't have reason to connect it until they reach the Dining Hall/Arena and find two grave urns sitting among the Court's own trophies.

> **Design Note.** This is a beat, not an encounter — no combat is required here, and it plays best if you let it breathe. Keep the prose gentler than the rest of the dungeon: less rot-and-decay horror, more hushed reverence. The contrast is doing narrative work — it reframes "corrupted grandeur" once the party sees what the grandeur was built on top of.

> **Serai Hook.** If Serai Thalos is in the party, her Anachronistic Knowledge background feature triggers here with no roll required: she immediately recognizes the hoofprint carvings, the open-air pyre-and-urn interment style, and that this predates the Court by a wide margin. This is her moment — let her carry the scene. A History check at her doubled proficiency (or Arcana/Religion, DM's call) can surface more: roughly how long ago, what the vine-and-hoof motifs signify, whether this was a large settlement or a small shrine.
>
> Don't confirm whether these were specifically her people. Let her be certain of the era and the culture, uncertain of the rest. That keeps her Bond ("someone else was also sent through time when I was — I need to find that person") open as a thread rather than closing it in a side room; it's a stronger beat for a later session than a one-shot payoff.
>
> If Serai isn't in the party, any character can piece this together with a DC 15 History or Religion check — just without the certainty or the color she'd bring to it.

**Treasure (The Urns).** Twelve sealed urns line the alcoves; opening one is free, but a proper search of its contents calls for an Investigation check. Roll or choose from the table below. Each urn yields one result, so a DM running this as a "search several urns" scene can call for the check once per urn opened.

| DC | Find |
|---|---|
| 8 | A scatter of worn silver coin, unfamiliar in mint (roughly 15 sp equivalent) |
| 10 | A single silver piece, tarnished black, tucked beside the ash |
| 12 | A Shabti funerary figurine, carved in the likeness of the deceased |
| 14 | A polished hematite or obsidian stone, worn smooth from handling (10 gp) |
| 16 | A Heart Scarab, carved bone or horn rather than beetle-shaped (18 gp) |
| 18 | A bloodstone or star rose quartz set in a thin gold wire (25 gp) |
| 20 | A carnelian pendant (25 gp) |
| 22 | A Potion of Healing (common; DMG 187), sealed in wax |

*The set-apart urn near the basin (see Serai Hook, above) holds ash only — no item. Whatever was buried with this occupant mattered more for who they were than what they owned.*

*The toll-coins.* The loose coin among the grave goods isn't stamped with any currency the party will recognize. A DC 12 History or Religion check identifies them as Charon's obols — coins left with the dead to pay passage across the boundary, not currency meant to be spent. Without the check, the party can still sense something's off (spending it feels wrong even before anyone says so out loud), but the check is what turns that unease into certainty. Either way, play what happens next as a roleplay beat rather than a mechanical restriction: let the party decide for themselves whether to take it.

> **Design Note.** This room predates the Court entirely, which makes it a clean seed if you're building toward Shaen Orvo's Persistent Nodes idea (see Turning This Into a Campaign) — a site with its own history the Court's corruption never touched, sitting quietly beneath the one that did.

> **Design Note.** If you want a longer dungeon, this is the easiest place to insert an extra level. The sewers can branch toward a second entrance or a smuggler's cache — the Sunken Hearth above already covers the "sealed chamber that predates the Court" idea.

## Part Three: The Court in Ruin (Dining Hall / Arena)

Other, smaller drains are impassable. The party may exit the sewers into the dining hall, where the next large drain is located, to explore the ruins of the court. There are hints throughout of the royals' perversions.

**Read-aloud — quick version:**
> Once the monarchs' great blood-sport arena, its stone benches now sag under decades of moss and silt. A long dining table sits set with corroded silverware, as if a feast were interrupted mid-course and never resumed.

*Expanded, if you have the room for it:* Once the monarchs' great blood-sport arena, its stone benches now sag under decades of moss and silt. The long dining table nearby is set with corroded silverware, as if a feast were interrupted mid-course and never resumed. Rusted manacles hang from the arena's support columns.

*DM note:* the mosaics clue from the Antechamber pays off directly here. This is what they depicted.

### Suggested Content

- A **Barbarian's hook** payoff: a single surviving ancestral trophy rack, empty but for one weapon. The ancestral blade the Fighter's warlord wants, or a hint toward it.
- One easy roleplay/investigation beat: a preserved menu or ledger (Investigation DC 12) naming the "guests" who never left, seeding NPC names.

`MODERATE`

**Undead Arena Champions.** The arena's dead answer the call to fight once more. Statues and shackled remains alike wrench free of moss and rust, taking up whatever's left of their old weapons (x2 champions). Fittingly for the room, they don't rise silently — the crowd's roar seems to echo with them.

*Statline:* reskin the two remaining Broken Guardians from the Antechamber (Animated Armor stat block, AC 18, HP 33 (6d8+6), Multiattack: two Slam attacks, 1d6+2 each) as arena champions rather than temple statues. Same stats, new flavor.

This is the party's second taste of the animated guardians — a full pair now, after the single warning shot in the Antechamber. Drawn by noise from the sewers onward.

> **Scaling Note.** Two champions swing from Moderate (party of 3, level 3) down to Easy (party of 6, level 5).
>
> | Party | Suggested Adjustment |
> |---|---|
> | 3 players, level 3 | Run as written. |
> | 4–5 players, any level | Add a third champion — keeps the "crowd's roar" flavor and the action economy honest. |
> | 6 players, or any level 4–5 | Add a third champion and combine with the Rot-Touched Hounds below rather than treating them as strictly alternates — the arena can support a bigger fight. |

`EASY`

**Rot-Touched Hounds (Optional/Alternate)**
Feral dogs that never left the arena, diseased and half-rotted but still hunting in a pack. Swap in for (or add alongside) the Arena Champions if you want a faster, bloodier fight. Use SRD **Wolf** as-is; the "rot-touched" flavor is cosmetic.

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| CR | 1/4 each |
| AC | 13 |
| HP | 11 (2d8+2) |
| Speed | 40 ft. |
| Attack | +4 to hit |
| Damage | 2d4+2 piercing |

**Pack Tactics.** Advantage on an attack roll against a creature if at least one of the hound's allies is within 5 ft. of the target and isn't incapacitated — already suits running 2–3 hounds together. A target knocked prone by a bite (DC 11 Strength save) is easy pickings for the rest of the pack.

From here, the party moves toward the throne room and the ritual hall beyond, leading finally to the crypt.

### The Inner Courtyard (Optional, off the Dining Hall)

A genuine branch, not a detour that gates progress — skip this room entirely if there's no Ranger or Druid in the party. A door off the Dining Hall, half-swallowed by ivy, leads out to what was once a formal courtyard. It didn't stay formal.

**Read-aloud — quick version:**
> Open sky, for the first time since the party went underground. A small wood has reclaimed the courtyard — real trees, real undergrowth, pushing up through cracked flagstones. It doesn't belong here, and it doesn't feel like it wants to.

*Expanded, if you have the room for it:* The corridor opens onto open sky — the first the party has seen since the antechamber. What was once a formal courtyard has become a small, wild glade: real trees, thick undergrowth, moss climbing walls that were clearly built to keep it out. Birdsong, faint and strange, drifts from somewhere in the canopy. The air smells of green growing things instead of rot, which after everything else in this dungeon feels almost more unsettling than reassuring.

**What's here.** At the glade's center stands a crimson stag — a genuine fae creature, its coat the color of old blood, antlers branching like cracked lightning. A manacle of cold iron rings its neck, chained to a stake driven into the earth. The stag doesn't struggle. It watches the party arrive with an intelligence that makes clear this is not an animal.

> **Ranger Hook.** This is the crimson stag the Ranger's hook describes. Legend undersold it — this isn't a monster to hunt, it's a captive to free or leave chained. Let the Ranger's player recognize what they're looking at (no roll needed if the hook is theirs); anyone can identify it as fae in nature with a DC 13 Nature or Arcana check.

### Freeing the Stag

The manacle is cold iron — a material that suppresses fae magic on contact, which is exactly why it's holding a creature this powerful. Removing it is simple in principle (it isn't locked, just heavy) and takes an action; no check required. The moment it comes off, the stag doesn't run. It changes.

**Read-aloud:**
> The crimson coat brightens, the cracked-lightning antlers steady, and for a moment the stag isn't an animal at all — it's a door. A figure steps through where the stag stood, tall and unhurried, dressed like the forest itself decided to attend court. **Oberon** inclines his head to the party, unbothered, as if he'd been expecting them.

**Oberon's Offer.** He is courteous, faintly amused, and entirely uninterested in violence — he came for his stag, not a fight, and he'll say as much if asked. He offers a boon in thanks, tailored to whoever's actually in the room:

- **If a Warlock is present** (particularly one bound through the Raven Queen, like Malrik Dhoran), Oberon offers to cleanse the corruption from their patron's connection and transfer their pact to him instead — Hexblade to Archfey. He's honest about the terms: the cleansing takes time. He can begin the working now, but it completes in **a year and a day**. The Warlock can accept the offer today and carry the promise forward, or decline outright. There's no take-back once accepted, and no rush to decide — Oberon is patient in the way only an immortal can afford to be.
- **If a Druid is present**, Oberon already knows what's wrong with this place. He offers the means to cleanse the Ritual Hall's altar — not just permission, but the actual method: a working, or a small vial of something silver and cold, whichever you'd rather hand over. This is the concrete "how" behind the Druid's altar-cleansing hook (see the Ritual Hall, below); without this, the Druid can still attempt the DC 15 check unassisted, but Oberon's gift removes the roll entirely and makes the healing certain rather than merely possible.
- **If neither is present**, Oberon still offers something — a minor boon to the party as a whole (a Potion of Superior Healing, a single-use ward against the Court's undead, or a favor redeemable later in a longer campaign). He doesn't leave empty-handed generosity on the table; it's a matter of pride.

> **Design Note.** Play Oberon as genuinely unbothered by the Court, the dungeon, or anything the party has done so far — he's older than all of it and treats the whole affair as faintly beneath him. That tone is the point: after three parts of rot, sconces, and undead, a fae lord who's mildly delighted to have his stag back is a real tonal breath, the same way the Sunken Hearth was.

### If the Party Attacks

Oberon does not fight. He was never going to. If the party attacks the stag, attacks him, or tries to force the manacle off in a way that reads as hostile rather than freeing, he simply leaves — stepping back through the same door he arrived by, the crimson stag at his side, gone before anyone can land a blow. He takes no offense he'll act on personally; he just declines to stay somewhere he's not welcome.

He doesn't leave the party nothing, though. Oberon travels with an attendant — a fey creature bound to his court and, unlike him, entirely willing to fight. He leaves her behind on his way out, a parting consequence rather than an accident: she steps from among the trees where the stag stood, and she has no interest in letting the party leave quietly.

## Part Four: Battle in the Crypt

This level contains the ruins of the throne room. It overlooks the dining hall and leads to a ritual hall where waking the preserved king and queen is depicted, and a crypt lair where the lich king and vampire queen await the rising of the next age of darkness.

### The Ritual Hall

Blood-crusted grooves score the black granite altar at the room's center — an altar to **Orcus**, Prince of Undeath, and the source of the Court's corruption. If the party carries any hook that involves the altar (Cleric, Paladin, Warlock), this is where it pays off. The altar offers exactly two live interactions: **feed it**, or **cleanse it**. A party that does neither can simply walk past — nothing forces engagement.

> **DM Only — Table Safety Check-In.** Feeding the altar is the module's heaviest optional content: a real offer to permanently transform a PC into a vampire or lich, and a possible scenario where party members are pressured or compelled to attack one another. None of it triggers without a player choosing to bleed on the altar. Before you run this scene, it's worth a quick out-of-character check with your table (or at least the player whose character is closest to the Cleric/Paladin/Warlock hook) that permanent character transformation and PvP-adjacent stakes are something they want. If not, the party can still interact with the altar meaningfully by cleansing it instead — see below — or simply leave it alone.

### Feeding the Altar

The altar answers blood, not intent. A single drop is enough to trigger it — there's no verbal negotiation, no "are you sure" prompt. Offering blood *is* the agreement, and the character doing it should understand that going in, even if their player doesn't fully grasp what they're agreeing to yet. Make sure the read-aloud makes this unambiguous before anyone rolls or narrates cutting themselves.

**Read-aloud:**
> The grooves in the altar aren't decoration. They're old blood, and they're waiting for more. Whatever this is meant to do, it wants something living to start.

The moment blood touches stone, the ceiling above the altar **opens** — not physically, but like a curtain drawn back. A portal, or perhaps only a very old mirror, shows a realm of bone and rot far away. Something vast looks back through it. It does not need to come closer to be terrifying.

> **Orcus's Offer.** **Orcus** speaks through the portal — he never physically enters the room; he doesn't need to. He is amused, patient, and utterly certain of the outcome. He offers the bleeding character a place among his favored: **remade as a vampire or a lich**, a small portion of his own dominion over death made theirs to keep. In exchange, he asks only that they carry his name into the world and spread death in it.
>
> This is not a trick dressed as a gift — it's a genuine offer, played straight. If the character accepts, the transformation is real, permanent, and mechanically significant. See **Accepting the Pact**, below.

### Accepting the Pact

If a player wants this for their character, treat it as a real alternate character path, not a punishment:

- **Vampire.** Reskin the transformation using the SRD **Vampire Spawn** as a mechanical floor — the character keeps their class and levels, but gains the Spawn's Regeneration, a bite attack that deals necrotic damage and grapples, and the standard vampiric weaknesses (sunlight, running water, no reflection). Work with the player on how much of this replaces their existing kit versus layers on top; this is a session zero-style conversation, not something to resolve in the middle of combat.
- **Lich.** Reskin using the SRD **Mage** the same way Malqor Veyth is built (see The Crypt, below) — archmage energy in an undead husk, not the full CR 21 Lich block. The character keeps their class; they gain a phylactery (a real, findable object now, and a real vulnerability later), resistance to necrotic damage, and immunity to being frightened.
- Either way, this is a campaign-level commitment, not a one-shot flourish. If you're running this as a standalone session, talk to the player about what happens after the table stands up — whether this becomes session one of something longer, or whether you handle the transformation narratively and move on.

### If the Party Splits

This is the scenario the table-safety check-in above exists for. If one or more characters accept Orcus's offer and others in the party refuse or object, Orcus is direct about what happens next: **his new servant proves their devotion by dealing with the others.** He orders them killed on the altar — sacrificed the same way they were, willing or not.

- If the newly-turned character (with or without help) succeeds in killing a party member on the altar, that character doesn't simply die — they rise again as a mindless zombie, bound to the altar's will rather than Orcus directly. This is a real, permanent loss of that PC as written; talk to that player about a replacement character or an NPC ally filling the gap.
- If the rest of the party wins the fight instead — killing or subduing the newly-turned character before Orcus's order is carried out — the pact is broken, but the altar remains active and furious. **Cleanse it quickly** (see below) or Orcus will retaliate: raising the recently dead nearby, or sending a wave of undead after the party in a future session as an escalating grudge.

*You do not need to force this confrontation into the same combat round as the offer. It's fine — often better — to let the tension sit for a scene or two before it turns violent, especially if the party is still arguing about what just happened.*

### Cleansing the Altar

The alternative to feeding it is shutting it off for good. Cleansing doesn't just tidy up the corruption — done right, it **consecrates the altar and locks Orcus out of it entirely**, permanently closing the portal/mirror above it. This is the Druid's hook payoff, and it's also open to a Cleric or Paladin who'd rather consecrate the altar than feed it.

> **Druid / Cleric / Paladin Hook.** A 10-minute ritual, no roll needed if a Cleric or Paladin performs it (a Consecrate-style rite is exactly their wheelhouse), or DC 15 Religion or Nature for a Druid working alone. If the party received **Oberon's gift** in the Inner Courtyard, this requires **no roll at all** — his gift is specifically built to make this certain. The immediate effect is quiet: the blood-crusted grooves lighten, the air in the room eases, and the portal above the altar seals shut, this time for good.
>
> The real payoff is downstream and slow, particularly for the Druid — the corruption spreading into the natural world (their hook) begins to heal once its source is cleansed rather than fed. Tell that player plainly: this is the moment their hook resolves, even though the change won't be visible until later, outside the dungeon.

Beyond the altar itself, the room hasn't been picked clean: warped shelves line one wall, a scorched flask sits where it was left, and a lantern long since out of oil still hangs from a bracket. Worth a look before the party moves on.

`DEADLY`

**The Ossyrran Blade**
A longsword hilt, rare, requires attunement (see below). While grasping the hilt, you can use a bonus action to cause a blade of solidified energy to spring into existence, or make the blade disappear. While the blade exists, this magic longsword has the finesse property. If you are proficient with shortswords or longswords, you are proficient with this weapon. You gain a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls made with this weapon.

**The hilt itself is inert.** A plain, unremarkable grip that could pass for mundane. Its power comes entirely from a single crystal socketed into the pommel, and attunement is to the crystal, not the hilt. A creature attuned to a crystal can freely move it between any compatible hilt; a creature holding the hilt without being attuned to its crystal gains none of the weapon's benefits, though the blade will still spring into existence for them (dim, guttering, deals no damage). Enough to identify what they're holding without letting an unattuned wielder use it.

**Swapping crystals.** Removing a crystal from a hilt takes an action and doesn't damage it. Attuning to a different crystal follows the normal attunement rules. The creature must end a short or long rest focused on the new crystal, and can only be attuned to one Ossyrran crystal at a time.

| Crystal | Damage Type | Secondary Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Black | Necrotic | +1d8 necrotic against living creatures; the wielder can't regain hit points while attuned |
| White | Radiant | +1d8 radiant against undead; sheds sunlight in a 15-ft radius |
| Red | Fire | +1d8 fire against constructs; on a critical hit, ignites flammable objects in a 5-ft radius |
| Green | Psychic | +1d8 psychic against beasts and monstrosities; target has disadvantage on its next Wisdom save |
| Purple | Poison | +1d8 poison against creatures without immunity to poison; target has disadvantage on its next Constitution save |
| Cyan | Force | +1d8 force against incorporeal creatures; ignores half and three-quarters cover |

**The Chest — The Treasury.** Bound in blackened iron, sealed beside the dais as a monarch's grave goods would be. Inside, resting in fitted velvet slots clearly built to hold more than what remains: five uncut crystals (white, red, green, purple, and cyan) alongside 300 gp and 75 pp in mixed coin and loose gems (roughly 75 gp).

**Quick Escape.** From the crypt, the party may find a secret, hidden exit that shortcuts them to the outside. Useful if you want a "grab the win and run" ending rather than a full clear.

> **Design Note.** Don't let the module force a clean kill. If Malqor or Seraphyne is reduced to 0 HP but the party doesn't destroy the phylactery / the coffin isn't staked-and-burned, either monarch can return later as a recurring antagonist. Perfect if you want this one-shot to become session one of something longer.

### The Web Underneath the Hooks

Garrick, Tey'orah, and Vess already have a live secret at the table. Vess recognizes Tey'orah as the "drunk priestess from the alley" she indirectly wronged, and is keeping quiet to avoid Garrick's fury. Selvos half-admires, half-resents Tey'orah's sheltered upbringing without having ever met her. Korr and Selvos have a warm, respectful history.

A second cluster runs through Kaelor, Malrik, Thane, Sarthos Kain, and Zyra. An information network, a battlefield alliance, and a shared devotion to the Raven Queen, all independently drawn to the same dungeon. None of this needs to surface in a one-shot, but if you run this as session one of a campaign, it's already loaded.

### The Raven Queen Pattern

Kaelor, Malrik, and Zyra are each tied to the Raven Queen independently — Kaelor as sworn devotee, Malrik through his Hexblade pact, Zyra through her Divine Soul bloodline. Three pregens bound to the goddess of fate and endings, converging on a court built to cheat death, reads as more than coincidence. Worth deciding ahead of time whether you want the table to notice.

> **Design Note.** The Vess/Tey'orah secret and the Raven Queen convergence can each carry an entire arc on their own. Pick one to surface first, or let them collide.

## Wrap-Up

The table leaves the crypt. Malqor and Seraphyne may be destroyed, driven off, or merely disturbed. The immediate hooks resolve (warlord paid off, altar cleansed or fed, guild debt settled), but the larger premise doesn't have to end. The Court's corruption was strong enough to raise two rulers from beyond death; it's reasonable that it isn't finished.

### Turning This Into a Campaign

- **If the boss survives:** Malqor's phylactery or Seraphyne's true coffin is still out there. A sequel adventure is a hunt for it.
- **If Shaen Orvo's pregen was in the party:** her "Persistent Nodes" research reframes the whole dungeon as one node among several across the multiverse. The natural next arc is chasing the pattern to wherever it repeats next.
- **If Lethari's pregen was in the party:** the Court's corruption "spreading into the natural world" gives you an overland arc. Blighted beasts and blackened vines lead to wherever you want to take the party next.
- **If the Vess/Tey'orah secret is still buried:** let it surface in a quieter, character-focused session before the next dungeon crawl.
- **If the Raven Queen pattern went unnoticed:** a later session can reveal that Kaelor, Malrik, and Zyra were each nudged toward this dungeon by the same divine hand. This raises the question of what the goddess of endings wanted with the Undying Court in the first place.
- **Loose threads to seed now, pay off later:** the warlord's other lost ancestral blades; the thieves' guild's other rumored vaults; Selvos's unfinished copy of the *Odyssey of the Dragonlords*; Sarthos Kain's double life; whoever built the Court's crystal-powered sconces and whether that knowledge still exists elsewhere.
