On the Binding of Elemental Crystal

The red crystal that powers this palace's old defenses is not, as the household staff believed, merely a light source. It is a conduit — raw elemental substance, uncut, that can be bound into a vessel and made to answer a wielder's will.

I have proven this much: the crystal accepts a working, once properly prepared, and will hold a single elemental affinity for as long as it remains bound. Six affinities have shown promise under study — necrotic, radiant, fire, psychic, poison, and force — though I suspect others exist that I have not had cause to test.

The Vessel

The hilt is the easy part. Any well-balanced grip will do; the crystal does the work, not the metal around it. The difficulty is the socket — it must be cut to the crystal's exact geometry, or the binding will not hold past the first working.

I have not solved the socket. I know it can be done — the old records make that much clear — but the precise cut eludes me. [The next several lines are illegible — water damage.]

Unfinished

The notes end mid-sentence. Whatever came next, its author never got to write it down.
