How to Use This Booklet

This is built to run as a single satisfying session. Descent, dungeon, boss, done. Every section is also written so you can keep pulling threads afterward. Look for Design Note call-outs throughout; they mark places where the one-shot quietly opens a door into something longer, or explain the reasoning behind a room, encounter, or choice.

Session lengthOne 3–4 hour sitting
ToneGothic ruin-crawl. Sith relics, undead royalty, corrupted grandeur.

Scaling This Module

This module is written for 3–6 players, level 3–5 — but a party of 3 level 3s and a party of 6 level 5s are not the same fight, even against identical stat blocks. Look for the Scaling Note callouts throughout; they call out the encounters where this matters most and give concrete adjustments per party size and level.

SituationGeneral Approach
Small party (3), low level (3)The module is tuned for this end of the range. Run encounters mostly as written; the boss fight and both sconce encounters are the ones to watch — they're the module's real difficulty spikes at this tier.
Large party (5–6), low level (3)More actions per round than the encounters expect. Add one extra combatant to group fights (Guardians, Champions) rather than buffing individual stat blocks — keeps the action economy honest without inflating any single monster's HP.
Any size, higher level (4–5)Static DCs and flat damage numbers age fast. Raise save DCs by 2–3, add a damage die to hazards (sconces, traps), and let Malqor use his higher-level spell slots — he's already statted as a 9th-level caster on the Mage block, so the tools are there.
Large party (5–6), higher level (4–5)Combine both adjustments above. This is the module's easiest matchup by a wide margin as written; without adjustment, most encounters (including the boss) drop from Deadly/Hard to Easy.
The Premise

The Undying Court was built by a King and Queen who foresaw the rising of the light and wished to prepare for it. It was both a grand palace and dangerous fortress. They repurposed their palace into a tomb so they might rise again when the balance of the dark began to overpower the light once again. Over the years, their oppressive, corrupted grandeur has turned to filth and ruin.

They chose undeath to master the dark side and cheat their own mortality. The Court styled itself as Dark Side adepts — Sith, in all but name — who mastered undeath itself as a weapon in their pursuit of immortality. Lichdom and vampirism weren't curses that befell them; they were the final rites of that mastery, chosen and pursued.

The party has their own reasons for descending — see the Rumor/Hook Ideas below, one per class, already written to slot straight into your pregens. Whatever brought them here, they all converge on the same partially-collapsed entrance.

Design Note

Every hook above survives the dungeon. The warlord still wants that blade. The guild still wants its cut. The dark power that whispered to the Warlock doesn't go silent just because the module ends.

Design Note

Payoff locations, at a glance: Barbarian and Fighter — Dining Hall trophy rack and the Ossyrran Blade (the Crypt). Rogue, Wizard, Sorcerer — the Treasury and/or Malqor's Study. Monk — the Treasury's scroll. Cleric, Paladin, Warlock, Druid — the Ritual Hall altar (fed or cleansed). Ranger, and Warlock/Druid again — the Inner Courtyard, off the Dining Hall (Part Three). Bard is intentionally the loosest hook in the set; Serai Thalos's own pregen Bond does the heavy lifting for her specifically, via the Sunken Hearth.
